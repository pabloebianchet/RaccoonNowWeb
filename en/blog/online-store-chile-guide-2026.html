How to open an online store in Chile in 2026: a step-by-step guide

Nov 25, 2026 · 6 min read · Raccoon Now
Raccoon Now · Blog

Opening an online store in Chile in 2026 means first sorting out registration with the SII, then the payment methods platforms and banks now require, and only then logistics. Chile added two key changes this year: a requirement to prove business registration before marketplaces and banks will process your payments, and a new data protection law that starts applying in December. This guide walks through the steps in the order that makes sense to tackle them.

1. Register with the SII: it's no longer optional

To sell legally in Chile you need to complete "Inicio de Actividades" with the SII, Chile's tax authority — the step that lets you issue receipts and invoices. You can do this as an individual, without setting up a formal company. The change that matters this year: since January 2, 2026, marketplaces, payment processors, banks and government bodies must verify proof of business registration before enabling payments or authorizing transactions. The SII also launched a portal called "Mi Negocio Cumple" to simplify the process using your tax ID and government digital key. In practice, you can no longer sell informally and collect payments through a gateway without being formally registered.

2. 19% VAT and mandatory electronic receipts

Chile's VAT (IVA) is 19% and applies to the vast majority of goods and services sold online. If you sell regularly, the SII requires an electronic receipt for every sale to a consumer, and an electronic invoice if you're billing other businesses, with an extra verification step at the SII. One recent change worth knowing if you import goods to resell: as of late 2025, the 19% VAT also applies to international purchases — from AliExpress, Shein or Amazon — without the exemption threshold that used to apply, which partly levels the field for local sellers.

3. Payment methods: Webpay leads, but it's not the only option

Webpay Plus, run by Transbank, is still the most widely used payment gateway at checkout in Chile. But it's no longer the only option that matters: Mercado Pago has grown as a digital wallet alternative, Flow has positioned itself for subscription and software businesses, and Khipu enables direct bank transfers, cheaper for higher-ticket purchases. Fees and settlement times vary between providers, so it's worth comparing more than one before integrating checkout.

4. Shipping: national couriers and urban last-mile delivery

For deliveries within Chile, Chilexpress, Correos de Chile, Starken and Blue Express cover most regions, with delivery times that vary between the Santiago metro area and more remote zones. If you sell on Mercado Libre, Mercado Envíos simplifies logistics by integrating directly with the marketplace. Decide from day one who covers the shipping cost and state it clearly on the product page.

5. Right of withdrawal: 10 days you have to respect (or 90)

Chile's consumer protection law (Ley N°21.398 and its amendments) gives buyers a 10-day right of withdrawal from the moment they receive a product, letting them return it without giving a reason. What many stores don't realize: if you don't send written confirmation of the purchase terms — price, delivery time, return policy — that window extends to 90 days. There are exceptions for goods that by their nature can't be returned, set out by decree. Spelling this out clearly on your site builds trust with a buyer who doesn't know you yet.

6. The new data protection law already has a date

Chile has a new personal data protection law (Ley N°21.719) that, according to the official timeline, takes effect on December 1, 2026 — although there's a bill in Congress to push that back a year. For an online store, what matters in practice is having a clear privacy policy, asking for explicit consent before using a buyer's data for marketing, and knowing that small businesses get a 12-month grace period with written warnings before fines of up to 4% of annual revenue.

What this means for your business

None of these steps is complicated on its own, but the order matters: sorting out business registration and electronic receipts before choosing a platform saves you from redoing that part later, and adding more than one payment gateway from day one keeps you from losing sales.
