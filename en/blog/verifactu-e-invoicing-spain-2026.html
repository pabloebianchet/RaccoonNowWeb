Verifactu in Spain: what it is, when it's mandatory, and how to avoid fines of up to €50,000

Nov 16, 2026 · 6 min read · Raccoon Now
Raccoon Now · Blog

If your business invoices in Spain — whether you're self-employed, a small business, or a corporation — there's a date you should already have on your calendar: during 2027, using invoicing software compliant with Verifactu, Spain's new digital invoicing standard, becomes mandatory. This isn't a VAT reform or a new tax — it's a technical requirement for how the software that issues your invoices has to work internally. And the fines for non-compliance can reach €50,000 per fiscal year.

What exactly is Verifactu?

Verifactu is the common name for Spain's regulation on requirements for computerized invoicing systems, approved by Royal Decree 1007/2023. In plain terms, it requires every invoicing system to generate a secure, tamper-evident record of each invoice issued, with a digital fingerprint that makes it impossible to delete or alter invoices without leaving a trace. If a system sends those records to Spain's Tax Agency (AEAT) in real time — the Verifactu mode proper — the invoice carries a QR code and the visible legend VERI*FACTU. Systems that don't submit data in real time still have to meet the same traceability requirements, just under a different mode of the regulation.

The real timeline: who must comply, and from when

The deadline has moved more than once. The original calendar pointed to 2026, but Royal Decree-Law 15/2025 — validated by Spain's Congress on December 11, 2025 — set the current deadlines: companies must have a Verifactu-compliant system in place from January 1, 2027, while self-employed workers, professionals, and other obligated taxpayers (individuals with business income, income-attribution entities) have until July 1, 2027. It's worth checking the Tax Agency's website before planning anything, since the topic keeps generating debate among accountants' associations and small-business groups asking for more time.

The fines: up to €50,000 per fiscal year

Article 201 bis of Spain's General Tax Law sets fines of up to €50,000 per fiscal year for anyone who produces, markets, or uses invoicing software that doesn't meet the regulation's requirements, or that allows invoice records to be altered. The amount isn't tied to your company's revenue — it applies per non-compliant system, which makes it especially harsh for small businesses still invoicing with spreadsheets or outdated software.

How to check whether your software already complies

One source of confusion: Spain's Tax Agency does not certify, approve, or homologate any invoicing software, and it doesn't publish any official list of valid programs. What the regulation actually requires is that each software provider file a compliance declaration with the AEAT confirming their product meets the technical requirements. If your invoicing provider markets its software as approved, ask to see that specific declaration, not a generic badge. And if your business runs invoicing through custom-built software or spreadsheets, start evaluating the migration now: adapting an ERP or in-house system to meet these traceability and integrity requirements isn't a one-afternoon fix.

What to check this week

You don't need to solve everything at once, but you do need a plan: first, confirm whether your business falls under the company deadline (January 2027) or the self-employed and professional deadline (July 2027). Second, ask your current invoicing provider, in writing, for their Verifactu compliance declaration. Third, if they can't produce one, start pricing alternatives early, well before demand piles up near the deadline. Fourth, if your business manages invoicing inside an ERP or custom system, add this requirement to your next development roadmap.

This article is general information and doesn't replace advice from a Spanish accountant or tax advisor for your specific situation, especially since the timeline could still shift.
